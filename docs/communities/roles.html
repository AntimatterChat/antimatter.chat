Roles and permissions

How to make community admins, create roles with their own colours and permissions, let people pick roles themselves, and group people in teams. A table at the end sums up who can do what.

The built-in roles

Every community has the same built-in roles. Their permissions come from the server's permission scheme, which only a system admin can change.

RoleShown asWhat it can do by default
System adminSystem Admin chipEverything, in every community, and the System Console.
Community adminCommunity Admin chipThe community's settings, roles, onboarding and layout; moderating and removing members; deleting anyone's messages; converting channels to private; archiving the default channels; webhooks and slash commands.
Memberno chipJoining public channels, creating public and private channels, inviting people. In the channels they are in: posting, and also renaming, archiving and managing members.
GuestGuest chip and tagOnly the channels they were added to.
Channel adminChannel Admin chipManaging one channel, its permissions and its banner.

These defaults suit a workplace, where people are trusted to organise their own channels. In a public community you will usually want members to do less: ask your system admin to adjust the permissions for your community in System Console › User Management › Permissions, where a Team Override Scheme can apply to your community only.

Make someone a community admin

  1. Open the community menu and choose Manage members.
  2. Find the person, open the menu next to their role, and choose Make Team Admin. (This dialog still says "team" for community.)

To take the role back, choose Make Team Member. Only community admins and system admins can do this: no custom role can make someone a community admin.

Keep at least two community admins, so that the community isn't stuck if one of them leaves.

Custom roles

Custom roles are roles you create for your community, as in Discord: "Moderators", "Staff", "Beta testers", "Contributors". A role can give people a colour, a group of their own in the member list, and permissions. Each role can also be mentioned, which notifies everyone who has it.

To manage roles, open the community menu, choose Organise the community, then Roles…. Community admins and people with the Manage roles permission see this item.

Create a role

  1. In the Roles dialog, choose Create role.
  2. Enter a Name.
  3. Pick a Colour, or type a colour code.
  4. Leave Show members separately on if the role's online members should get their own group in the member list.
  5. Turn on Anyone can take it if members may give themselves this role (see Roles anyone can take).
  6. Turn on the Permissions the role should give (see the table below).
  7. Under Members, add people with Add people of the community….
  8. Choose Create role.

In the list of roles, Move up and Move down set the order. The order matters: a person's highest role sets the colour of their name, and it decides whom they can manage (see The order of roles). Delete the role deletes it at once, without asking for confirmation.

The permissions a role can give

SwitchWhat it allows
AdministratorThe community settings, onboarding, layout, member attributes and category permissions, and editing any role. People with it are not affected by channel permissions and can't be timed out.
Manage rolesCreating and editing the roles below their own, and giving them to people. Also creating and editing teams.
Invite peopleAdding people to the community.
Remove peopleRemoving people from the community, and timing out the people whose roles are below theirs.
Create public channelsCreating channels anyone in the community can join.
Create private channelsCreating channels for the people invited.
Manage channelsRenaming, converting and archiving channels, managing their members, and setting their permissions.
Manage messagesEditing and deleting other people's messages.
Manage integrationsWebhooks and slash commands, their own and other people's.
  • Administrator doesn't include Manage roles or Remove people. For someone who should run the community with you, turn on all three, or make them a community admin.
  • A role can only add permissions. Invite people and the two Create switches are already given to every member by default, so they only matter if your system admin took them away from members.
  • Someone who isn't a community admin can only give a role the permissions they have themselves.

A moderators role

For a public community, a typical "Moderators" role has Manage messages, Remove people and Manage channels, and is shown separately with its own colour. The "Moderators" role that the layout presets create lacks Remove people: add it if your moderators should time people out or remove them.

The order of roles

Roles are ranked by their place in the list, the highest first, as in Discord:

  • People with Manage roles can only edit, move and give the roles below their own highest role, and create new roles below it.
  • People with Remove people can only time out people whose highest role is below theirs.
  • Roles that anyone can take don't count for the rank.
  • Community admins are above every role. They can't be timed out.
The rank isn't checked when removing someone from the community: anyone with Remove people can remove anyone, including people above them. Give Remove people only to people you trust.

Give someone a role

There are three ways:

  • In Roles…, edit the role and add the person under Members.
  • In the person's mod view, in the Roles section, use Add a role, or the × on a role to remove it (see The mod view).
  • Through the onboarding, for roles anyone can take (see Onboarding).

Only members of the community can have its roles. Someone who leaves the community loses its roles, and doesn't get them back if they return.

Roles anyone can take

A role with Anyone can take it is open to all: members give it to themselves, or drop it, from the community menu under Your roles…. This item only appears when the community has such roles. Use them for interests or notification groups, for example "Announcements", "Linux users" or "Paris meetups", which people can mention.

A role anyone can take still gives its permissions to whoever takes it. Don't turn on permissions in such a role unless you want every member to have them.

Colours, groups and badges

At the top of the Roles dialog, community admins have two switches for the whole community:

  • Role colours: names take the colour of the person's highest role. On by default.
  • Everyone shows a team badge: people who chose no badge show the tag of their first team in this community. Off by default; useful in a company, where the badge says which department someone works in.

In the member list, each role shown separately gets its own group of online members, the highest role first. When no role is shown separately, the list groups admins and moderators under Admin and Moderators. Members can switch the list between Roles and Teams. A system admin can turn off grouping by role on the whole server, with Group members by role in the System Console.

Teams inside a community

In Antimatter, a team is a group of people inside a community, such as a department, a project or a working group. A team has a name that can be mentioned, a short tag (up to 8 characters, for example "DET"), a lead, a description, and a team channel. Members can show the tag of one of their teams next to their name.

Create a team

  1. Open the community menu and choose Teams…. The dialog that opens still says "User Groups".
  2. Choose Create Group, enter the team's name and its mention name, add its members, and create it.
  3. To set its tag, lead and channel, click the team's tag next to one of its members' names to open its card, then choose Edit the team. Fill in Tag, Lead, Team channel and What the team does, and save.

Community admins and people with Manage roles create and edit teams. The lead has no extra rights: the title is informational. Each member chooses which team badge to show, in their settings under My account, Team badge.

The member list can group people by team: choose Teams under Group members by at the top of the list.

Permissions in one channel

Roles apply in every channel of the community. To give a role more or fewer rights in one channel or one category, use channel permissions: see Channel permissions. A private channel can also be kept for some roles only: its members then follow the roles automatically.
