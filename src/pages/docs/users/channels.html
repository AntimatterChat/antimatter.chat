Communities

A community is a space with its own channels and members: your company, a department, a club or a public project. You can belong to several communities on the same server. They are shown as round icons in the rail on the far left of the window. A dot on an icon means unread messages; a number counts the mentions of you.

Switch between communities

Click a community's icon in the rail. Antimatter opens the channel you were last in. From the keyboard, press Ctrl+Alt+Down or Up for the next or previous community, or Ctrl+Alt+1 to 9 for the first to ninth (⌘+⌥ on a Mac).

Join another community

  1. Click Add a community (the + under the community icons).
  2. Choose Join another community. A page lists the communities open to you.
  3. Pick one to join it.

Some communities can only be joined with an invitation: ask one of its members or admins for an invite link. If your server lets you, the same menu also offers Create a community.

The first time you open some communities, a Welcome to … dialog asks you a few questions. Your answers give you the roles and channels that fit you. You can Skip it.

The community menu

Click the community's name at the top of the sidebar, or right-click its icon in the rail, to open its menu. Depending on your rights, it offers:

  • Browse channels, to find channels to join.
  • Mark all as read (also Shift+Esc).
  • Mute community, which mutes all its categories, and Unmute community.
  • Show unread channels only (also Ctrl+Shift+U) and Hide muted channels.
  • Notification settings, which opens your notification settings.
  • Invite people, when you may invite.
  • Your roles…, when the community has roles anyone can take: they colour your name, and may let you do more.
  • Teams…, the community's teams, each with a tag, a lead and a channel.
  • View members.
  • Create channel and Create category.
  • Add to folder (from the rail only), see Community folders.
  • Leave community.

Community folders

If you belong to many communities, you can group their icons into folders in the rail:

  • Drag a community's icon onto another one to put them together in a folder. Drag icons up or down to reorder them.
  • Or right-click a community's icon, choose Add to folder, then a folder or New folder with another community.
  • Click a folder to open or close it. Right-click it for Mark folder as read, Folder settings (its Name and Colour) and Remove folder; the communities stay in the rail.

Folders are your own: nobody else sees them.

Leave a community

Choose Leave community in its menu and confirm. You leave all its channels. To come back later, you may need a new invitation.

Channels

A channel is a conversation about one subject, open to the community's members. Public channels have a # before their name; anyone in the community can find and join them. Private channels have a lock, and only their members can see them. Voice channels have a speaker: see Voice channels.

Every community has a default channel that everyone is in, called General. Some communities put everyone in all their channels.

Find and join channels

  1. Open the community menu and choose Browse channels.
  2. Type in Search channels, or pick a kind: All, Public, Private, Archived or Ask to join. Tick Hide joined to see only the channels you are not in.
  3. Click Join next to a channel.

Some private channels can be found but not joined directly: click Ask to join to send a request to their admins. The button then reads Requested; click it again to withdraw your request. You are added when an admin accepts.

You can also join a channel from a link to it, or by typing /join #channel-name.

Create a channel

  1. Choose Create channel in the community menu, or click the + next to a category in the sidebar.
  2. Pick the Channel type: Text (messages, files and threads) or, when available, Voice channel.
  3. Type a Name.
  4. Choose the Category it goes in.
  5. Turn on Private channel if only invited members should see it.
  6. Click Create channel.

More options… opens the full dialog, with the channel's address and purpose. Whether you may create public or private channels depends on your community's settings.

Add people to a channel

Choose Add members in the channel's menu, or click Add people in its information. Search by name or @username, select people, and click Add. They see the channel's history and get notified.

Channel information

Click the channel's name at the top of the conversation to see About it: its purpose and header, who created it and when, and buttons to Favourite, Mute, Add people, Copy link and open its Settings. Links lead to its Members, Pinned messages and Files. The header next to the name is a short text set by the channel's members; click Show the whole header if it is cut.

The header can also show Has guests (guests can read and post in this channel), Translated, Restricted (an access policy decides who can join) or that the channel is shared with other servers.

The member list

Click Member list (the two people at the top right) to show or hide the channel's members on the right. They are grouped by role, then online and offline. Use Group members by to group them by Roles or Teams instead, unless your community's admins chose the grouping for everyone, and the filter to show people with a given attribute, such as a site. Click someone to see their profile card, or right-click them for their menu. With the Simplified interface on (see Accessibility), the member list stays closed until you open it.

Leave a channel

Choose Leave channel in the channel's menu. For a private channel, Antimatter asks you to confirm, since you need an invitation to come back. You can't leave the community's default channel. In a community where everyone is in all channels, the menu offers Mute channel instead.

Archive a channel

Archiving a channel ends its conversation but keeps its history. If you may, choose Archive channel… in the channel's menu, or Archive channel in its settings. In a community set up as a workplace, you can archive the channels you are an admin of, such as those you created; in other communities, only the community's admins and the roles that manage channels can.

An archived channel can still be read, but nobody can post, react or pin there. Its admins can still add people to it, for example someone who needs to read its history. Choose Close channel in its menu to leave it.

Organise your sidebar

The sidebar lists the current community's channels in categories. Some categories are set up by the community's admins for everyone; the others are yours, and only you see them.

The channel menu

Point at a channel and click Channel options (⋯), or right-click it. You'll find:

  • Mark as read or Mark as unread.
  • Favourite or Unfavourite: favourite channels go to the Favourites category.
  • Mute channel: For 1 hour, For 8 hours, For 24 hours, For a week or Until I unmute it. A muted channel shows no unread badge and sends no notifications; it shows a bell with a line through it. Unmute channel undoes it.
  • Notification preferences…: see Notifications for one channel.
  • Move to…: put the channel in another category, one of Yours, or one For everyone if you are an admin.
  • Hide until new messages: the channel moves to a Hidden section at the end of the sidebar until someone writes in it. Show in the sidebar brings it back.
  • Copy link, Open in new window and Export channel… (see below).
  • Add members, Channel settings, Leave channel, and the actions of apps.

Categories

  • To create one, choose Create category in the community menu, type a Name and click Create category.
  • Drag channels from one category to another, or within a category to reorder them. Drag categories to reorder them too.
  • Click a category's name to fold or unfold it.
  • Click Category options (⋯) next to its name to Rename category, Delete category (its channels go back to the default categories), Mute category, Mark category as read, Sort its channels (Alphabetically, By recent activity or Manually), Move up or Move down, or Create new category.

Categories set up by the admins say so when you point at their name. When such a category is Locked, its channels stay in it.

Unread channels

  • A channel with unread messages is shown in bold; a number counts the mentions of you.
  • When unread channels are out of view, a More unreads button appears at the top or bottom of the list: click it to scroll to them.
  • Turn on Group unread channels in Settings › Appearance to gather them in an Unreads section above the categories.
  • Press Ctrl+Shift+U to show only unread channels, and click Show all to see them all again.
  • Alt+Up and Alt+Down go to the previous or next channel; add Shift to go to the previous or next unread one.

Topics

Topics, the threads that someone gave a name, show under their channel in the sidebar, for the people who follow them, with the number of new replies. Click one to open it. See Make a thread a topic.

Export a channel

Choose Export channel… (or Export conversation…) in the menu to download its messages as a CSV file you can open in a spreadsheet: who wrote them, when, and their replies. Pick the Messages to include: Last 30 days, All time or Custom dates, then click Export. Attachments are listed by name, and deleted messages are left out. Your administrator may keep this to admins.

Direct and group messages

Direct messages are private conversations between you and one other person; group messages include up to ten people, you included. They belong to no community: the same conversation shows whichever community you are in.

Find your conversations

Your recent conversations are listed under Open conversations at the bottom of the sidebar: the one you are in, then the latest active ones, as many as fit. An unread conversation has a red ring around its picture and a small grey count. To see them all, click All direct messages (the chat bubble at the bottom of the community rail). The sidebar then lists every conversation, latest first, with the last message. It also has links to your Mentions, Followed threads, Saved messages, Drafts, Scheduled messages and Notes to self.

Unread conversations that don't fit in Open conversations wait as round pictures under the chat bubble, at the bottom of the community rail, with their counts. Click one to open the conversation.

Open conversations lists 10 conversations at most by default: change it with Open conversations in Settings › Appearance (5, 10, 15, 20 or 40). To remove a conversation from the list, point at it and click Close, or choose Close conversation in its menu.

Start a conversation

  1. Click All direct messages, then type a name in Find or start a conversation and press Enter. If you already have a conversation with that person, it opens; otherwise a new one starts.
  2. Or click New message (the + at the top of that list), or press Ctrl+Shift+K: in the Direct Messages dialog, pick one or more people and click Go. Several people make a group message.

You can also click someone's name or picture and choose Message.

In a direct message

The header shows the other person's status, their local time, and when they were last online. Above the message box, Antimatter warns you when they have Do not disturb on, are out of office, or may be asleep.

Group message options

In a group message's menu:

  • Add people… starts a new group with everyone in it and the people you add. The old group stays as it was.
  • Rename group gives the group a name that only you see. Leave it empty to show the members' names again.
  • Convert to private channel turns the group into a private channel of a community, when the group has grown into a project.
  • Edit header, Mute conversation and Notification preferences… work as in channels.

Messages from people you don't know

Depending on your privacy settings, direct messages from people you haven't talked with yet may wait as message requests, without notifying you. See Message requests.
