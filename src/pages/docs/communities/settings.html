Open the community settings

Open the community menu: click the community's name at the top of the channel list, or right-click its icon in the community rail. Then choose Community settings. Only community admins (and system admins) see this item.

The settings dialog has these tabs:

TabWhat it holds
OverviewName, description and icon.
AccessPublic or private, allowed email domains, the invitation code.
Team Membership and Channel MembershipMembership rules based on people's attributes. These tabs only appear when a system admin has turned on attribute-based access control.
ChannelsEveryone in every channel, and the default channel.
AutolinksText patterns that become links in messages.
Some tabs come from the classic interface and still say "team": "Team Name", "Public Team", "Team Icon". In Antimatter, a team and a community are the same thing.

Other community-wide choices are in the community menu, under Organise the community: roles (see Roles and permissions), onboarding and member attributes (see Members and onboarding), and the community layout (below).

Name, description and icon

On the Overview tab:

  • Team Name is the name shown in the community rail and at the top of the channel list. It can have 2 to 64 characters.
  • Description helps people pick the right community when they browse the ones they can join. It can have up to 50 characters.
  • Team Icon is the picture in the community rail: a BMP, JPG or PNG file. Without one, the rail shows the community's initials. Remove image removes it at once.

Save your changes with the bar at the bottom of the dialog. The community's address (the part of the URL after the server's name) is chosen when the community is created and can't be changed afterwards.

Who can join

On the Access tab:

  • Discoverability: a Public Team appears in the list of communities that anyone with an account on the server can browse and join. A Private Team can only be joined with an invitation. Switching from public to private creates a new invitation link and stops the old one from working.
  • Allow only users with a specific email domain to join this team: when this is checked, only people whose email address ends with one of the domains you list can join, for example example.org. Separate several domains with spaces or commas. This suits a company workspace; leave it off for a public community.
  • Invite Code: the code in the community's invitation link. Regenerate creates a new link and makes the previous one stop working. Do this if an invitation link was shared where it shouldn't have been.

If the community's members come from linked groups (a directory such as LDAP, set up by a system admin), the Access tab says so, and these choices are replaced by the group sync.

Joining also depends on how the server is set up: whether people can create an account at all, and from which email domains. These are server settings; ask your system admin (see What to ask your system admin).

Communities have no member limit by default. A system admin can set one for every community on the server (Max Members Per Community, in System Console › Site Configuration › Users and Teams); once a community reaches it, nobody else can join until someone leaves.

Everyone in every channel

On the Channels tab, Members are in every channel decides how channel membership works in your community.

  • On: every member is in every public channel, and in the private channels that their roles open. Nobody joins or leaves channels: people mute the channels they don't want to follow. The channel menu shows Mute channel where Leave channel used to be, and moderators can't remove anyone from a single channel. To keep someone out, remove them from the community, time them out, or change the channel's permissions.
  • Off: people join the channels they want, can leave them, and channel admins can add and remove members.

Guests and bots are never added automatically: they are only in the channels they are added to. Private channels that are not opened by a role (invite-only channels) are not affected either.

The switch is saved as soon as you change it. Turning it on adds everyone to the channels they may see; on a large community this takes a little while. Turning it off keeps everyone where they are, and they are free to leave.

New communities have this switched on, unless the system admin changed that default (New communities put everyone in every channel in the System Console). Communities created before this setting existed have it off until you turn it on.

For a company workspace with many teams and projects, off is usually better: people only see the channels of their work. For a small public community, on keeps everyone in the conversation.

The default channel

The default channel is where newcomers land, and where the community's join and leave messages go. Everyone is in it and can't leave it. When a community is created, its default channel is Town Square.

To choose another one, use Default channel on the Channels tab, or open a public channel's menu and choose Make it the default channel. The default channel must be a public channel that isn't archived. Everyone is added to the new default channel.

Town Square, Off-Topic and the default channel can be archived, but only by community admins. If the default channel is archived, the community has no default channel any more: the setting shows An archived channel, and people land in the first channel they are in until you choose another one. When Town Square is gone, the list offers None: the first channel each person is in.

Autolinks

Autolinks turn text in messages into links as they are posted. A typical use is issue keys: LUM-12 becomes a link to that issue in your tracker. They apply to the messages posted in your community, in every app, notification and search, because the server rewrites the message before saving it. Code, links and images are left alone.

On the Autolinks tab:

  1. Choose Add an autolink. Start from a ready-made pattern (issue keys, pull requests, merge requests, CVE identifiers) or from Blank.
  2. Give it a name, for example "Jira issues".
  3. In Pattern, write a regular expression, for example (PROJ-\d+).
  4. In Becomes, write what the match turns into. $0 is the whole text that matched, and $1, $2 and so on are the pattern's groups. For example: [$1](https://issues.example.com/browse/$1).
  5. Choose where it applies: All channels, or only some channels.
  6. Optionally, check Whole words only, and Bots' messages too if messages posted by bots should be linked as well.
  7. Type an example in Try a message to see the result, then choose Save.

Each autolink has an On box to pause it without deleting it. Link edited messages too applies the autolinks to messages when they are edited; otherwise only new messages are linked. A community can have up to 100 autolinks.

Community layout, presets and templates

A layout is a community's structure: its categories for everyone and the channels in them, its channels, its roles and their permissions, and its onboarding. In the community menu, choose Organise the community, then Community layout….

Three presets are offered:

PresetWhat it sets up
Hybrid (recommended)A locked "Information" category with Town Square, an open "Community" category where members file their own channels, and a "Moderators" role.
CommunityCategories that only admins arrange ("Information" and "Channels"), and a "Moderators" role.
WorkplaceAs in Mattermost: everyone arranges their own categories. It removes the categories for everyone; roles stay.

The "Moderators" role of the presets can edit and delete other people's messages and manage channels and their members. It can't remove or time out people until you add Remove people to it (see Roles and permissions).

The first time a community admin opens a new community that has no categories for everyone and no roles, Antimatter offers the presets once, under Set up followed by the community's name. Choose Not now to skip.

Copy a layout to another community

  1. In the community you want to copy, open Community layout… and choose Export as a template. Your browser downloads a JSON file.
  2. In the other community, open Community layout…, choose Apply a template… and pick the file.

Applying a preset or a template adds the channels and roles that are missing, updates the roles that have the same name, files the channels into the categories, and replaces the onboarding. It removes nothing except the categories for everyone that the template doesn't include. The export only includes the channels you are a member of, and no messages.

Settings that are not the community's

  • Direct messages and message requests: whether direct messages from strangers arrive normally or as requests is each member's own choice, which they can make per community in their settings, under Privacy & safety. Community admins can't set it for them (see Ignoring and blocking).
  • Grouping members by role in the member list, custom emoji, the apps, and the member limit are set for the whole server by a system admin.

Leaving and deleting a community

Anyone can leave a community with Leave community at the bottom of the community menu. Community admins can't archive or delete a community from Antimatter's interface: a system admin does it in System Console › User Management › Teams, by opening the community and choosing Archive Team.
